# Documents, translations and legalisation

This guide describes the documents a Bachelor's application often asks for, sworn translation in Morocco, and how a document is made valid abroad. It does not replace the official pages. Every statement links to its source, and anything we could not confirm is marked as to be checked.

## The documents to gather

The list below gathers the items the destinations ask for most often. It is an overview. The list that counts is the one for the country and programme you choose.

- The bac certificate or the bac diploma.
- Transcripts from your secondary school years, or the full bac transcript.
- Birth certificate.
- Passport or national identity card.
- Identity photos in <bdi>ICAO</bdi> format.
- A language certificate.
- A motivation letter.
- Proof of funds or of financial support.

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.maroc.campusfrance.org/system/files/medias/documents/2026-07/D%C3%A9pliant%20%C3%89tudier%20en%20France%20site.pdf" verified="2026-10-11">
For a <bdi>Campus France</bdi> application, your identity document, your school history and diplomas, and a French certificate or a convocation to the exam, are asked for when you submit the file.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://france-visas.gouv.fr/etudiant" verified="2026-10-11">
For France, files submitted from 1 August 2026 must include proof of funds of at least 877.50 EUR per month.
</Fact>

## Sworn translation in Morocco

In Morocco, the official translation of a document is done by a sworn translator accredited to the courts.

<Fact kind="official" source="https://atajtraduction.ma/" verified="2026-10-11">
Sworn translators accredited to the courts are listed by the <bdi>ATAJ</bdi> association, which is accredited by the Ministry of Justice of the Kingdom of Morocco. Only these translators are authorised to translate official, legal, school, university and administrative documents.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://atajtraduction.ma/" verified="2026-10-11">
Translations made by these translators are printed on official green paper, with a green relief stamp at the bottom left of each sheet.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://atajtraduction.ma/" verified="2026-10-11">
The King's Prosecutor authenticates the sworn translator's signature on translations meant for a State that is not a party to the Hague Convention.
</Fact>

The fee for sworn translation and the legal text that governs the profession could not be confirmed from an official source. This point is to be checked.

## Morocco and the apostille

Morocco is a party to the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961, which removes the requirement of legalisation for foreign public documents between States parties.

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.hcch.net/en/news-archive/details/?varevent=513" verified="2026-10-11">
Morocco acceded to the Hague Convention on 27 November 2015. The Convention entered into force for Morocco on 14 August 2016, which made Morocco the 110th Contracting State.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/?cid=41" verified="2026-10-11">
In the Conference status table, Morocco appears as a party, with a deposit on 27 November 2015 and entry into force on 14 August 2016. The deposit type shows that at least one other State raised an objection.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/notifications?csid=1321&disp=type" verified="2026-10-11">
Germany raised an objection to Morocco's accession on 14 June 2016, under article 12, paragraph 2, of the Convention. On the information available, the Convention therefore does not operate between Germany and Morocco.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.apostille.ma/fr/presentation.aspx" verified="2026-10-11">
Morocco's apostille portal confirms the deposit of the accession instruments on 27 November 2015 and the entry into force of the Convention on 14 August 2016 between Morocco and its States parties.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://assets.hcch.net/docs/7a7b8c62-e93e-4fe8-a84c-0d74af1abca1.pdf" verified="2026-10-11">
Beyond the Convention, Morocco states that some foreign public documents are also exempt from legalisation under bilateral instruments, including a 1997 convention with Spain and a 1981 protocol with France.
</Fact>

## The legalisation chain

There are two paths. If the destination is a party to the Convention with no objection and the document is public, a single apostille replaces the chain. Otherwise, and for documents that are not public, legalisation goes signature by signature up to the destination's consulate.

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.hcch.net/fr/states/authorities/details3?aid=1044" verified="2026-10-11">
In Morocco, the apostille is issued by court officers for acts that come from a court or a judicial officer, and by local authorities at province or prefecture level for the other public documents.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.apostille.ma/fr/presentation.aspx" verified="2026-10-11">
Morocco's portal also names the Secretary General of the Ministry of Justice for acts of that ministry's central administration, and notaries for the acts they draw up.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.consulat.ma/en/legalization-ministry-foreign-affairs-and-international-cooperation" verified="2026-10-11">
The offices of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs legalise the signatures of public officials, of the authorities of the Ministries of the Interior and of Justice, and of the diplomatic agents and foreign consular officers accredited in Morocco.
</Fact>

<GuideSteps>
  <li>Have the document issued, or notarised if its nature requires it.</li>
  <li>Have the document translated by a sworn translator.</li>
  <li>Have the translator's signature authenticated by the King's Prosecutor when the destination is not a party to the Hague Convention.</li>
  <li>Have the relevant signatures legalised by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.</li>
  <li>Have the document legalised last by the destination's consulate or embassy in Morocco.</li>
</GuideSteps>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://rabat.diplo.de/ma-fr/service/1693714-1693714" verified="2026-10-11">
Germany's embassy in Rabat notes that a translation is only asked for at legalisation if the consular officer does not read Arabic, the document's language, so it can be useful to have the document translated before legalisation.
</Fact>

## What each destination asks for

Each country adds its own items and its own translation rule.

### France

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.maroc.campusfrance.org/les-procedures-de-campus-france-maroc" verified="2026-10-11">
Both the application and the pre-consular stage run on the <bdi>Études en France</bdi> platform. <bdi>Campus France Maroc</bdi> is the only official point of contact recognised for these steps.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.maroc.campusfrance.org/system/files/medias/documents/2026-07/D%C3%A9pliant%20%C3%89tudier%20en%20France%20site.pdf" verified="2026-10-11">
Unless exempt, the file asks for a French certificate or a convocation to the exam. The titles named are <bdi>TCF TP</bdi>, <bdi>DELF B2</bdi> or <bdi>DALF</bdi>.
</Fact>

### Germany

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.uni-assist.de/en/how-to-apply/assemble-your-documents/translations" verified="2026-10-11">
For Germany, <bdi>uni-assist</bdi> asks for diplomas in their original language, with a translation into German or English. Non-certified translations from an ordinary agency are not accepted.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.uni-assist.de/en/tools/info-country-by-country/details-country/country/ma/" verified="2026-10-11">
For a Moroccan diploma, <bdi>uni-assist</bdi> asks for the bac transcript, and notes that the bac alone does not allow you to study directly in Germany.
</Fact>

Germany's objection to Morocco's accession matters for this country. It should be checked whether the objection has been withdrawn before treating an apostille as enough for Germany. This point is to be checked.

### Netherlands

<Fact kind="official" source="https://studyinnl.org/plan-your-stay/checking-your-diploma" verified="2026-10-11">
For the Netherlands, the institution decides, after consulting <bdi>Nuffic</bdi> on the value of the diploma.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.nuffic.nl/en/education-systems/morocco/level-of-diplomas" verified="2026-10-11">
According to <bdi>Nuffic</bdi>, Morocco's bac attestation corresponds to at least a Dutch <bdi>HAVO</bdi> diploma. The technical bac corresponds to a <bdi>HAVO</bdi> with vocational subjects.
</Fact>

For the Netherlands, whether the bac must be legalised or apostilled for <bdi>Studielink</bdi> or <bdi>Nuffic</bdi>, and which translation language is accepted, could not be confirmed on an official page. This point is to be checked.

### Hungary

<Fact kind="official" source="https://stipendiumhungaricum.hu/documents/stipendium_hungaricum/2025-2026/document_for_applicants_ba_ma_otm_2025.pdf" verified="2026-10-11">
For Hungary, any document that cannot be provided in the language of the chosen programme or in Hungarian must be translated. The translation is submitted with a copy of the original document.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.ttk.pte.hu/en/admission/scholarships/stipendium-hungaricum/" verified="2026-10-11">
A <bdi>Stipendium Hungaricum</bdi> application asks for, among other items, a motivation letter, proof of language level, school certificates and their translations, the transcript and its translation, a copy of the identity document and a medical certificate.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://stipendiumhungaricum.hu/documents/stipendium_hungaricum/2025-2026/document_for_applicants_ba_ma_otm_2025.pdf" verified="2026-10-11">
The motivation letter asked for is at least one page and must be written in the language of the chosen programme or in Hungarian.
</Fact>

### Italy

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.universitaly.it/studenti-stranieri" verified="2026-10-11">
For Italy, visa pre-enrolment for non-EU students happens only on the <bdi>Universitaly</bdi> portal, which is free and official.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.unimi.it/sites/default/files/2026-07/Documenti%20Universitaly%2026-27%20ENG.pdf" verified="2026-10-11">
The secondary-school diploma is translated into Italian by an official translator and legalised by the authorities of the country that issued it. Some institutions also accept the diploma in French, English, German or Spanish.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.unifi.it/en/study-us/your-learning-path/registration-and-enrolment/international-students/study-titles-obtained" verified="2026-10-11">
The <bdi>dichiarazione di valore in loco</bdi> is an informative document issued by an Italian representation abroad. It describes the value of the diploma in the country where it was obtained, and does not amount to automatic recognition.
</Fact>

<Fact kind="official" source="https://www.cimea.it/EN/pagina-attestati-di-comparabilita-e-verifica-dei-titoli" verified="2026-10-11">
Instead of the <bdi>dichiarazione di valore</bdi>, <bdi>CIMEA</bdi> can issue a comparability and verification statement. These statements do not bind the institution.
</Fact>
